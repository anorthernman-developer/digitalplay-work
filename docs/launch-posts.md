## 2. Why "digital play"

**X**

> The name's borrowed from my brother Graham's book Digital Play (with Kyle Mawer), about using games to teach languages. Its big idea: people learn best when they're playing. I've taken that out of the classroom and into the arts.
>
> https://digitalplay-work.netlify.app/book.html

**Substack note**

> A bit of family history behind the name. Digital Play is a book by Kyle Mawer and my brother Graham Stanley, about using computer games in language teaching. It won an ELTon for innovation back in 2012.
>
> The idea I took from it is simple. Play isn't a break from learning, it's one of the best ways to do it. And what you do with the tech matters far more than how fancy the tech is.
>
> digitalplay.work points that idea at music, art, design, code and writing. I've written a short summary of the book in my own words on the site, with a link if you want to buy it.

## 4. Loose parts (featuring the Loose Parts Table)

**X**

> In 1971 the architect Simon Nicholson argued that places full of movable bits make people more creative than places where everything's fixed. So the first art tool is a table of loose shapes. Shake it, shove things about, colour it from your own photo.

**Substack note**

> Why "Loose Parts Collective"?
>
> In 1971 the architect Simon Nicholson wrote about the theory of loose parts. The idea is that a place full of movable, open-ended bits (sticks, blocks, fabric, water) invites far more creativity than one where everything is bolted down. Adventure playgrounds owe him a lot.
>
> Every tool on digitalplay.work is meant to be a tray of loose parts. The first art tool takes it literally. It's a table of circles, arches, sticks and triangles. Shake it, push it about until a picture turns up, pull colours from your own photo, and export it as a PNG or SVG.
