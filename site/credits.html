The book

The name and the spirit of the project come from Digital Play: Computer games and language aims by Kyle Mawer and Graham Stanley (Delta Publishing, 2011). Our summary page is written in our own words.

Ideas we built on

The starter tools are built on old, openly shared ideas. Each one says what inspired it:

  • Dada chance poems made by drawing words from a hat (1920); The cut-up method developed in Paris around 1959
  • Early text adventure games of the mid-1970s; Classroom use of text adventures for reading and writing
  • Mid-century modernist poster design; Classical page proportions
  • Simon Nicholson's theory of loose parts (1971); Friedrich Fröbel's play gifts (1830s)
  • The Game of Life, published in 1970; Early cellular automaton research from the 1940s
  • The Logo programming language, first written in 1967; Floor-turtle robots used in schools in the 1970s and 80s
  • The colour wheel, first drawn in the early 1700s; Bauhaus colour courses (1920s); WCAG contrast guidance
  • The kaleidoscope, patented in 1817; Rangoli and kolam threshold drawings; Islamic geometric pattern

Simon Nicholson’s theory of loose parts (1971) gave the collective its name.

Accessibility

We aim for WCAG 2.2 level AA: readable contrast, keyboard access to every control, visible focus and respect for reduced-motion settings. If something gets in your way, tell us on GitHub.
