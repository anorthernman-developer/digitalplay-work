Loose Parts Collective · free, open and yours to change

Creative tools to play with, made to be pulled apart.

digitalplay.work is a free collection of small browser toys for music, art, design, code and words. Bring your own sounds, photos and sentences, push them about, and see what turns up. No sign-up, no feed, and most of them work offline.

How it works

Every tool follows the same three habits.

  1. Bring your own stuff

    Each tool starts with your material: a voice memo, a photo off your phone, a paragraph you wrote on the bus. The tool is the table; you bring the parts.

  2. Learn as you go

    Every tool has a LEARN panel that explains the idea behind it in plain words, where it came from, and a couple of things to try next.

  3. Keep it, share it, change it

    Save presets, export them as a file, and hand them to a mate. Every tool is a single HTML file under an open licence, so you can open it up and make it your own.

Where the name comes from

“Digital play” is the title of a 2011 book by Kyle Mawer and Graham Stanley about using computer games in language teaching. Its big idea is simple: people learn well when they are playing, and a teacher can build real learning around real games. This site takes that idea out of the language classroom and into the creative arts. Read our summary of the book.
