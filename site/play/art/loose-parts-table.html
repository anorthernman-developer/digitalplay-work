Learn · Loose Parts Table

What it does

A tabletop of loose shapes you push around until a picture turns up. Add circles, arches, sticks and triangles, colour them from your own photo, shake the table and keep what works.

Loose parts

In 1971 the architect Simon Nicholson argued that the more loose bits a place has, the more people invent there. A pile of crates beats a finished climbing frame because you decide what it becomes. This table works the same way: nothing is fixed, and the picture is whatever you leave behind.

Play blocks

In the 1830s and 1840s the German teacher Friedrich Fröbel, who started the first kindergarten, gave children simple wooden spheres, cubes and tiles to arrange. Many twentieth-century designers grew up with those blocks, and you can see them in a lot of modern geometric art.

Three things to look for

  • Balance. A big shape on one side can be balanced by two small ones on the other.
  • Negative space. The gaps are shapes too. Try leaving half the table empty.
  • Odd numbers. Three or five of something usually feels livelier than two or four.

Try this

  1. Press Shake, then move only three pieces until it feels right.
  2. Load a photo of your street with Your photo and use its colours.
  3. Make a face using exactly five pieces.
  4. Turn on Tidy and build something that only uses the grid lines.

Keys

  • Delete remove the selected piece
  • D duplicate
  • R / Shift+R turn 15°
  • + / − bigger / smaller
  • Arrow keys nudge
  • F bring to front
  • S shake the table
  • L this panel · P presets
