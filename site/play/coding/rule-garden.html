Learn · Rule Garden

What it does

Plant a few cells, set two tiny rules about birth and survival, and watch a living pattern grow. Swap rules, paint new seeds, or grow a garden from your own photo.

Small rules, big behaviour

A cellular automaton is a grid of cells that are either alive or not. Every tick, each cell looks at its eight neighbours and follows a rule. The famous one, published in 1970 by the mathematician John Conway (1937–2020), is written B3/S23: a dead cell is Born with exactly 3 live neighbours, and a live cell Survives with 2 or 3. That is the whole program, yet it makes gliders, oscillators and machines that build other machines.

Emergence

Nothing in the rule says "make a glider". The glider emerges from lots of cells following the same local rule. Flocks of birds, traffic jams and fashion work a bit like this too.

One-line mode

The elementary automata are even simpler: a single row, where each cell looks only at itself and its two neighbours. There are 256 possible rules. Rule 30 looks random and Rule 90 draws a Sierpiński triangle. Each new row is drawn underneath the last, so you see the whole history at once.

Try this

  1. Draw a row of three cells with Life and press Step a few times.
  2. Load a photo, seed from it, then try the Seeds rule. What survives?
  3. Type your own rule, such as B36/S125, and give it a name in Presets.
  4. In one-line mode, find a rule number that draws something you'd wear.
