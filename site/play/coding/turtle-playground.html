Learn · Turtle Playground

What it does

Steer a little turtle with a handful of words and it draws as it goes. Write your own commands, make them repeat, give them names, and watch shapes appear line by line.

Where turtles came from

Logo was written in 1967 by a small team at a Massachusetts research company, with the aim of letting children think like programmers by playing. In the early 1970s a robot "turtle" that drove across the floor with a pen underneath was added. Thousands of UK schools later had one on a classroom floor.

The words this turtle knows

fd 50 forward, bk 50 back, rt 90 right turn, lt 90 left turn, pu pen up, pd pen down, color red or color #2a46c9, width 4, home, repeat 4 [ fd 50 rt 90 ], random 100, and procedures:

to square :size repeat 4 [ fd :size rt 90 ] end then square 80.

Numbers can be sums too: fd :size * 2.

The big idea

The turtle only knows where it is and which way it faces. Every shape is a set of small steps from its own point of view, which is called body-syntonic thinking: you can act a program out by walking it. If a shape looks wrong, stand up and walk it.

Try this

  1. Draw a triangle. How far does the turtle have to turn at each corner? (Hint: not 60.)
  2. Make a procedure called star and use it five times in different places.
  3. Change rt 90 to rt 91 inside a big repeat and see what happens.
  4. Draw your initials.
