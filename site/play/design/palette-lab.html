Learn · Palette Lab

  

    
What it does
Build a colour palette from one colour you like or from a photo of your own, then check every pairing for readable contrast before you use it.

    
The colour wheel
Isaac Newton bent the spectrum into a circle in 1704, and painters have used wheels ever since. Harmonies are just shapes laid over the wheel: complementary uses opposites, analogous uses neighbours, triadic uses three points of a triangle, and split takes a colour plus the two either side of its opposite.
Contrast is not optional
The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) measure how much lighter one colour is than another, as a ratio from 1:1 to 21:1. Normal text needs at least 4.5:1 to pass level AA, and large text needs 3:1. The grid here checks every pair so you can see which colours can carry words and which should stay as shapes.
Why lightness matters most
Two colours can look very different and still have the same lightness, which makes text vanish for many people. A quick check: imagine the palette in black and white. If two swatches turn the same grey, don't put one on the other.

    
Try this
  1. Load a photo of somewhere you love and see what palette falls out.
  2. Lock one colour you hate, then make the rest work around it.
  3. Find a pair that passes AAA (7:1) and build a poster on just those two.
  4. Export the CSS and drop it into a page you are making.
